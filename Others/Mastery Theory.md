**Hard Skills vs Soft Skills**
We know that there are hard skills and soft skills. In my observations, hard skills take a long time to harness while soft skills are usually much easier and take less practice to be just be good at. 
Examples of hard skills: basically every technical skills: engineering, computer, mathematics, and so on. Examples of soft skills : communications, empathy, negotiation, and so on


**Practical skills (muscle memory) vs Theoritical knowledge (brain)**
It is very idiot to say that practical skills are much more important than theoritical knowledge. Both are equally neccessary to build the human civilization that we have now. Moreover, human was the first mammal that dominate the Earth since the era of Reptile not because of their muscle, but their brain volume.

Usually human gain mastery in practical skills by doing repititive movements, such as throwing a ball into the basket or playing the same piece over and over again on an instrument. It is hard, but you always know what to do. However, theoritical knowledge is different, you are not demanded to do things quickly, you just had to done it right and logical. This will bring you in a situation where you are just stuck and running out of idea on what to do next. It is very hard to overcome and frustrating. some skills acquired by a particular people usually can be judged right away as you saw them. For example, people who are good and talented in sports usually have good body posture and muscle ( even though there are also some of those who are skinny but extremely talented too), you can just guess that they should probably good in that area. while some other skills like music, and also soft skills, even theoritical knowledge aren't visible. but skills like music usually still relies more on muscle memory just like sports, whereas soft skills and theoritical knowledge relies more on brain memory which experience in doing them would enhance the speed and fluency of the skills that the person had. For example. if a person had spent so much time speaking in front of an audience, he/she should get better and better in doing a speech, also if someone had performed a lot of mathematics, calculations, he/she should be good in doing proofs, solving problems more quickly and accurately.

**Self study vs Mentorship**
I observed that some of my classmates skipped class just so that they had much more time studying on their on. The fact that they went to the most prestigiouso college in the country and they pay tuition is kinda funny when I realized this fact. 

I do think that self studying has its own pluses and minuses compared to mentored/tutored/lectured studying. This is some of the comparisons that I could summarized probably:

Self study :
- much more freedom, much more time to explore and learn at your own pace, no pressure (+)
- moody (-)
- no pressure could also mean you will not push yourself further (-)
- you don't know how well you compared to others, no discussions [sometimes] (-)
Lectured:
- much more oriented, you can check where you are because there are always test to assesed your skills/knowledge at some point (+)
- don't need motivation, need discipline
- has place to discuss, you know how good you are relative to your classmates

**Things you enjoy, easy vs Things you enjoy, hard to do**
It is easier to do things you enjoy (cheap dopamine), than to do things you enjoy but are hard to do, demanding, and not rewarding sometimes. This is because humans are more likely doing the instant result that could make them happy. But most of the things that are easy to do are not useful in long term, the hard ones are more important, it is often called passion, it is more likely to cause frustration, but the fruit from doing these hard-enjoyable things are unforgettable and useful. 

This is the problem most of the time why people that have passion are not neccesarily always happy pursuing them, because the results that they want are very hard to obtain. You need to learn the boring stuff, suffer enough to start master and do things effortlessly good, for example, like when you are mastering a language or programming language. If you are already on a level where you could improvise, create a problem by yourself, exploring, you will find the hard things you enjoy, will be more enjoyable and easier somehow. 



**learn thing fast vs slowly**
actually learning things fast and slowly has its own pluses and minuses. If you want to learn practical stuff, you could probably do it fast, don't do too much reading, just keep practicing. And even becoming a master would really take a long time for practical thing. 

But learning things slowly really keeps the knowledge into long term memory, better understanding, better comprehension, more detail, well basically overall much better. But I think the ideal way is to mix between the two sometimes.
