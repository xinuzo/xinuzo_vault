>[!tips] Theorem 5.1 Uniqueness of Identity and Inverse
>Assume that $G$ together with $*$ is a group.
>(a) The identity element of $G$ is unique. That is, if $e$ and $f$ are elements of $G$ such that $e*a=a*e=a$ and $f*a=a*f=a$ for each $a \in G$, then $e=f$.
>(b) Each element in a group has a unique inverse. That is, if $a, x, y \in G$, $e$ is the identity, and $a*x=x*a=e$ and $a*y=y*a=e$, then $x=y$.

>[!tips] Theorem 6.1 Symmetric Group
>The set of all permutations of a nonempty set $S$ is a group with respect to composition (called the Symmetric Group on $S$, $Sym(S)$).

>[!tips] Theorem 6.2 Order of $S_n$
>The order of $S_n$ is $n!$.

>[!tips] Theorem 6.3 $S_n$ Non-Abelian
>If $n \ge 3$, then $S_n$ is non-Abelian.

>[!tips] Property: Disjoint Cycles
>Disjoint cycles commute; that is, if $\alpha$ and $\beta$ represent disjoint cycles, then $\alpha\beta = \beta\alpha$.

>[!tips] Theorem 6.4 Cyclic Decomposition
>Any permutation of a finite set is either a cycle or can be written as a product of pairwise disjoint cycles; and, except for the order in which the cycles are written, and the inclusion or omission of 1-cycles, this can be done in only one way.

>[!tips] Property: Cyclic Decomposition of Conjugates
>If $\alpha, \beta \in S_n$, then the cyclic decomposition of $\beta \circ \alpha \circ \beta^{-1}$ can be found from the cyclic decomposition of $\alpha$ by replacing each number $k$ by the number appearing below $k$ in the two-row form of $\beta$. (Example 6.7)

>[!tips] Lemma 7.1 Properties of Subgroups
>Let $G$ be a group with operation $*$, and let $H$ be a subgroup of $G$.
>(a) If $f$ is the identity of $H$ and $e$ is the identity of $G$, then $f=e$.
>(b) If $a \in H$, then the inverse of $a$ in $H$ is the same as the inverse of $a$ in $G$.

>[!tips] Theorem 7.1 Subgroup Test
>Let $G$ be a group with operation $*$, and let $H$ be a subset of $G$. Then $H$ is a subgroup of $G$ iff
>(a) $H$ is nonempty,
>(b) if $a \in H$ and $b \in H$, then $a*b \in H$, and
>(c) if $a \in H$, then $a^{-1} \in H$.

>[!tips] Theorem 7.2 Alternating Group
>The set of all even permutations in $S_n$ forms a subgroup of $S_n$ for each $n \ge 2$. This subgroup is called the alternating group of degree $n$, denoted $A_n$. The order of $A_n$ is $\frac{1}{2}(n!)$.
