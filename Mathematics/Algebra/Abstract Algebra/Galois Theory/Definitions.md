>[!tips] 42.1 Simple Extension
>If $E$ is an extension field of $F$ and $E = F(\alpha)$ for some $\alpha \in E$, then $E$ is said to be a simple extension of $F$. The field $F(\alpha)$ is the smallest subfield of $E$ containing both $F$ and $\alpha$.

>[!tips] 42.1 Algebraic and Transcendental
>An element $\alpha \in E$ is algebraic over $F$ if $\alpha$ is a root of some nonzero polynomial in $F[x]$. If $\alpha$ is not algebraic over $F$, it is transcendental over $F$.

>[!tips] 42.1 Degree of an Extension
>The degree of an extension $E$ of a field $F$, denoted $[E:F]$, is the dimension of $E$ considered as a vector space over $F$. If the degree is finite, $E$ is a finite extension.

>[!tips] 43.1 Splitting Field
>An extension $E$ of $F$ is a splitting field for a polynomial $f(x) \in F[x]$ if $f(x)$ factors into linear factors in $E[x]$ and $E$ is generated over $F$ by the roots of $f(x)$.

>[!tips] 44.1 Galois Group
>The Galois group of an extension $E$ over $F$, denoted $\text{Gal}(E/F)$, is the group of all automorphisms of $E$ that leave every element of $F$ fixed.

>[!tips] 45.1 Algebraic Extension
>An extension $E$ of $F$ is an algebraic extension if every element of $E$ is algebraic over $F$.

>[!tips] 46.1 Separable Extension
>An extension $E$ of $F$ is separable if the minimal polynomial of every element in $E$ has distinct roots (no repeated roots) in a splitting field.

>[!tips] 47.1 Normal Extension
>An algebraic extension $E$ of $F$ is normal if every irreducible polynomial in $F[x]$ that has a root in $E$ splits completely into linear factors in $E[x]$.

>[!tips] 49.1 Solvable Group
>A group $G$ is solvable if there exists a finite sequence of subgroups $G = G_0 \supseteq G_1 \supseteq \dots \supseteq G_k = \{e\}$ such that each $G_{i+1}$ is a normal subgroup of $G_i$ and each quotient group $G_i/G_{i+1}$ is Abelian.

>[!tips] 49.1 Solvable by Radicals
>A polynomial $f(x) \in F[x]$ is solvable by radicals if all its roots can be obtained from $F$ by a finite sequence of additions, subtractions, multiplications, divisions, and root extractions (radicals).
