>[!tips] Theorem 3.1.1 Additivity of Binomials
>[cite_start]If $X_1, ..., X_m$ are independent binomial variables with parameters $(n_i, p)$, then $Y = \sum X_i$ is binomial $b(\sum n_i, p)$. [cite: 40]

>[!tips] Theorem 3.3.2 Sum of Chi-squares
>[cite_start]If $X_1, ..., X_n$ are independent $\chi^2(r_i)$ variables, then $Y = \sum X_i$ has a $\chi^2(\sum r_i)$ distribution. [cite: 42]

>[!tips] Theorem 3.4.1 Standard Normal Transformation
>[cite_start]If $X \sim N(\mu, \sigma^2)$, then $Z = (X - \mu)/\sigma \sim N(0, 1)$. [cite: 45]

>[!tips] Theorem 3.4.2 Sum of Independent Normals
>Let $X_i \sim N(\mu_i, \sigma_i^2)$ be independent. [cite_start]Then $Y = \sum a_i X_i$ has a Normal distribution $N(\sum a_i \mu_i, \sum a_i^2 \sigma_i^2)$. [cite: 46]

>[!tips] Corollary 3.4.1 Sample Mean Distribution
>[cite_start]If $X_1, ..., X_n$ is a random sample from $N(\mu, \sigma^2)$, then the sample mean $\bar{X}$ is $N(\mu, \sigma^2/n)$. [cite: 46]

>[!tips] Theorem 3.5.1 Linear Combinations of Multivariate Normal
>[cite_start]If $\mathbf{X}$ has a multivariate normal distribution $N_n(\mathbf{\mu}, \mathbf{\Sigma})$, then $\mathbf{Y} = \mathbf{C}\mathbf{X} + \mathbf{b}$ has a multivariate normal distribution $N_m(\mathbf{C}\mathbf{\mu} + \mathbf{b}, \mathbf{C}\mathbf{\Sigma}\mathbf{C}')$. [cite: 49]

>[!tips] Theorem 3.6.1 Student's Theorem
>Let $X_1, ..., X_n$ be a random sample from $N(\mu, \sigma^2)$. Then:
>1. $\bar{X}$ and $S^2$ are independent.
>2. $\frac{(n-1)S^2}{\sigma^2}$ has a $\chi^2(n-1)$ distribution.
>3. [cite_start]$\frac{\bar{X} - \mu}{S/\sqrt{n}}$ has a t-distribution with $n-1$ degrees of freedom. [cite: 53]
