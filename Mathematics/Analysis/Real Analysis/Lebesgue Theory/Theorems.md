>[!tips] Theorem 11.15 Egoroff's Theorem
>If $\{f_n\}$ converges to $f$ pointwise on a set of finite measure, it converges uniformly on a subset with arbitrarily small complement measure.

>[!tips] Theorem 11.24 Fatou's Lemma
>If $f_n \ge 0$ are measurable, $\int (\liminf f_n) \le \liminf \int f_n$.

>[!tips] Theorem 11.26 Monotone Convergence Theorem
>If $0 \le f_n \le f_{n+1}$ and $f_n \to f$, then $\int f_n \to \int f$.

>[!tips] Theorem 11.29 Lebesgue Dominated Convergence Theorem
>If $f_n \to f$ pointwise, and $|f_n| \le g$ where $g$ is integrable, then $\int |f_n - f| \to 0$ and $\int f_n \to \int f$.

>[!tips] Theorem 11.32 Comparison with Riemann
>If $f \in \mathscr{R}$ on $[a, b]$, then $f$ is Lebesgue integrable and the integrals agree.
>$f \in \mathscr{R}$ iff $f$ is continuous almost everywhere.

>[!tips] Theorem 11.38 $L^2$ Space
>The space of square-integrable functions $L^2(\mu)$ is a complete metric space (Hilbert space).
