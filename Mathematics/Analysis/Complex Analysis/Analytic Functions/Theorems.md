>[!tips] 2.22 Cauchy-Riemann Equations
>Suppose $f(z) = u(x, y) + iv(x, y)$ and $f'(z_0)$ exists. Then the first-order partial derivatives of $u$ and $v$ satisfy:
>$$u_x = v_y, \quad u_y = -v_x$$
>at $(x_0, y_0)$. Also, $f'(z_0) = u_x + i v_x$.

>[!tips] 2.23 Sufficient Conditions for Differentiability
>If $u(x, y)$ and $v(x, y)$ have continuous partial derivatives that satisfy the Cauchy-Riemann equations at $(x_0, y_0)$, then $f'(z_0)$ exists.

>[!tips] 2.25 Properties of Analytic Functions
>If $f'(z) = 0$ everywhere in a domain $D$, then $f(z)$ is constant in $D$.

>[!tips] 2.26 Harmonic Conjugates
>If $f(z) = u(x, y) + iv(x, y)$ is analytic in a domain $D$, then $u$ and $v$ are harmonic functions. $v$ is called a harmonic conjugate of $u$.
