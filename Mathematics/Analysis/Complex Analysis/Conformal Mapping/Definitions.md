>[!tips] 9.101 Conformal Mapping
>A transformation $w = f(z)$ is conformal at $z_0$ if it preserves the magnitude and orientation of angles between curves passing through $z_0$.

>[!tips] 9.101 Isogonal Mapping
>A mapping that preserves magnitudes of angles but not necessarily orientation (e.g., $w = \bar{z}$ reverses orientation).

>[!tips] 9.101 Scale Factor
>The linear stretching factor at $z_0$ is $|f'(z_0)|$.
>Areas are scaled by approximately $|f'(z_0)|^2$.

>[!tips] 9.104 Harmonic Conjugates Invariance
>If $u(x, y)$ is harmonic in a domain $D_z$ and $w = f(z)$ is analytic, then $U(u, v) = u(x(u, v), y(u, v))$ is harmonic in $D_w$ (under conditions). Actually, typically we transform boundary value problems where the potential $\Phi$ remains harmonic.
