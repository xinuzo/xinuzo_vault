
> [!tip] 12.1 Topological Space
> A topology on a set $X$ is a collection $\mathcal{T}$ of subsets of $X$ having the following properties:
> 1. $\emptyset$ and $X$ are in $\mathcal{T}$.
> 2. The union of the elements of any subcollection of $\mathcal{T}$ is in $\mathcal{T}$.
> 3. The intersection of the elements of any finite subcollection of $\mathcal{T}$ is in $\mathcal{T}$.
> A topological space is an ordered pair $(X, \mathcal{T})$ consisting of a set $X$ and a topology $\mathcal{T}$ on $X$.

> [!tip] 12.2 Open Set
> If $X$ is a topological space with topology $\mathcal{T}$, a subset $U$ of $X$ is an open set of $X$ if $U$ belongs to the collection $\mathcal{T}$.

> [!tip] 12.3 Discrete and Indiscrete Topologies
> * **Discrete Topology:** The collection of all subsets of $X$ (the power set).
> * **Indiscrete (Trivial) Topology:** The collection consisting only of $X$ and $\emptyset$.

> [!tip] 12.4 Finite Complement Topology
> Let $X$ be a set. The collection $\mathcal{T}_f$ consisting of all subsets $U$ of $X$ such that $X - U$ is either finite or all of $X$ is a topology on $X$, called the finite complement topology.

> [!tip] 12.5 Finer and Coarser Topologies
> If $\mathcal{T}$ and $\mathcal{T}'$ are two topologies on a given set $X$, and if $\mathcal{T} \subset \mathcal{T}'$, we say that $\mathcal{T}'$ is finer than $\mathcal{T}$, and that $\mathcal{T}$ is coarser than $\mathcal{T}'$.

> [!tip] 13.1 Basis for a Topology
> A basis for a topology on $X$ is a collection $\mathcal{B}$ of subsets of $X$ (called basis elements) such that:
> 1. For each $x \in X$, there is at least one basis element $B \in \mathcal{B}$ containing $x$.
> 2. If $x \in B_1 \cap B_2$, there is a basis element $B_3 \in \mathcal{B}$ such that $x \in B_3 \subset B_1 \cap B_2$.

> [!tip] 13.2 Topology Generated by a Basis
> The topology $\mathcal{T}$ generated by a basis $\mathcal{B}$ is defined as follows: A subset $U \subset X$ is open in $X$ if for each $x \in U$, there is a basis element $B \in \mathcal{B}$ such that $x \in B \subset U$.

> [!tip] 13.3 Standard, Lower Limit, and Upper Limit Topologies on $\mathbb{R}$
> * **Standard Topology:** Generated by the basis of all open intervals $(a, b)$.
> * **Lower Limit Topology ($\mathbb{R}_l$):** Generated by the basis of all half-open intervals $[a, b)$.
> * **Upper Limit Topology:** Generated by the basis of all half-open intervals $(a, b]$.

> [!tip] 14.1 Order Topology
> Let $X$ be a set with a simple order relation. Assume $X$ has more than one element. The order topology is generated by the basis consisting of:
> 1. All open intervals $(a, b)$ in $X$.
> 2. All intervals of the form $[a_0, b)$, where $a_0$ is the smallest element of $X$ (if it exists).
> 3. All intervals of the form $(a, b_0]$, where $b_0$ is the largest element of $X$ (if it exists).

> [!tip] 15.1 Product Topology on $X \times Y$
> Let $X$ and $Y$ be topological spaces. The product topology on $X \times Y$ is the topology having as basis the collection $\mathcal{B}$ of all sets of the form $U \times V$, where $U$ is an open subset of $X$ and $V$ is an open subset of $Y$.

> [!tip] 16.1 Subspace Topology
> Let $X$ be a topological space with topology $\mathcal{T}$. If $Y$ is a subset of $X$, the collection $\mathcal{T}_Y = \{Y \cap U \mid U \in \mathcal{T}\}$ is a topology on $Y$, called the subspace topology.

> [!tip] 17.1 Closed Set
> A subset $A$ of a topological space $X$ is said to be closed if its complement $X - A$ is an open set.

> [!tip] 17.2 Interior and Closure
> Let $A$ be a subset of $X$.
> * **Interior ($\operatorname{Int} A$):** The union of all open sets contained in $A$. It is the largest open set contained in $A$.
> * **Closure ($\overline{A}$):** The intersection of all closed sets containing $A$. It is the smallest closed set containing $A$.

> [!tip] 17.3 Neighborhood
> A neighborhood of a point $x$ is an open set $U$ containing $x$.

> [!tip] 17.4 Limit Point
> A point $x \in X$ is a limit point of $A$ if every neighborhood of $x$ intersects $A$ in some point other than $x$ itself.

> [!tip] 17.5 Hausdorff Space
> A topological space $X$ is called a Hausdorff space if for each pair $x_1, x_2$ of distinct points of $X$, there exist neighborhoods $U_1$ and $U_2$ of $x_1$ and $x_2$, respectively, that are disjoint.

> [!tip] 18.1 Continuous Function
> Let $X$ and $Y$ be topological spaces. A function $f: X \to Y$ is continuous if for each open subset $V$ of $Y$, the set $f^{-1}(V)$ is an open subset of $X$.

> [!tip] 18.2 Homeomorphism
> Let $X$ and $Y$ be topological spaces; let $f: X \to Y$ be a bijection. If both $f$ and $f^{-1}$ are continuous, then $f$ is called a homeomorphism.

> [!tip] 19.1 Product and Box Topologies (Arbitrary Products)
> Let $\{X_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in J}$ be an indexed family of topological spaces.
> * **Box Topology:** Generated by the basis of sets $\prod U_\alpha$, where $U_\alpha$ is open in $X_\alpha$ for every $\alpha$.
> * **Product Topology:** Generated by the basis of sets $\prod U_\alpha$, where $U_\alpha$ is open in $X_\alpha$ for every $\alpha$, and $U_\alpha = X_\alpha$ for all but finitely many values of $\alpha$.

> [!tip] 20.1 Metric and Metric Space
> A metric on a set $X$ is a function $d: X \times X \to \mathbb{R}$ such that:
> 1. $d(x, y) \ge 0$, and $d(x, y) = 0 \iff x = y$.
> 2. $d(x, y) = d(y, x)$ (Symmetry).
> 3. $d(x, z) \le d(x, y) + d(y, z)$ (Triangle Inequality).
> A metric space is a space $X$ equipped with a specific metric $d$.

> [!tip] 20.2 Metric Topology
> If $d$ is a metric on $X$, the topology induced by $d$ (the metric topology) is generated by the basis of all $\epsilon$-balls $B_d(x, \epsilon) = \{y \mid d(x,y) < \epsilon\}$ for $x \in X$ and $\epsilon > 0$.

> [!tip] 22.1 Quotient Topology
> Let $X$ be a topological space, $Y$ a set, and $p: X \to Y$ a surjective map. The quotient topology on $Y$ defines a subset $U \subset Y$ to be open if and only if $p^{-1}(U)$ is open in $X$.
