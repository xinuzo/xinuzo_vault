
> [!theorem] Theorem 74.1 (Standard Presentations of Surfaces)
> The standard quotient spaces formed from polygonal regions yield the following fundamental surfaces:
> 1. **Sphere ($S^2$):** $a a^{-1}$
> 2. **Torus ($T$):** $a b a^{-1} b^{-1}$
> 3. **Projective Plane ($P^2$):** $a a$
> 4. **Klein Bottle ($K$):** $a b a b^{-1}$

> [!theorem] Theorem 75.1 (Connected Sum of Projective Planes)
> The connected sum of two projective planes is homeomorphic to the Klein bottle:
> $$P^2 \# P^2 \cong K$$

> [!theorem] Theorem 75.2 (Connected Sum of Torus and Projective Plane)
> The connected sum of a projective plane and a torus is homeomorphic to the connected sum of three projective planes:
> $$P^2 \# T \cong P^2 \# P^2 \# P^2$$

> [!theorem] Theorem 77.5 (The Classification Theorem for Closed Surfaces)
> Every closed surface is homeomorphic to exactly one of the following spaces:
> 1. The sphere $S^2$.
> 2. The connected sum of $n$ tori: $T \# T \# \dots \# T$ (an orientable surface of genus $n \ge 1$).
> 3. The connected sum of $m$ projective planes: $P^2 \# P^2 \# \dots \# P^2$ (a non-orientable surface of genus $m \ge 1$).

> [!theorem] Theorem 78.1 (Euler Characteristics of Standard Surfaces)
> The Euler characteristics of the classified closed surfaces are topological invariants and are given by:
> 4. $\chi(S^2) = 2$
> 5. $\chi(T \# \dots \# T) = 2 - 2n$ (for $n$ tori)
> 6. $\chi(P^2 \# \dots \# P^2) = 2 - m$ (for $m$ projective planes)

> [!theorem] Theorem 78.2 (Invariance of Euler Characteristic)
> If $X$ and $Y$ are closed surfaces, then $X$ and $Y$ are homeomorphic if and only if they have the same Euler characteristic $\chi$ and they are either both orientable or both non-orientable.
