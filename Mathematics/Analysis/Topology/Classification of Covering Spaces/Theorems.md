# Theorems.md

> [!theorem] Theorem 79.1 (The General Lifting Lemma)
> Let $p: (E, e_0) \to (B, b_0)$ be a covering map. Let $f: (Y, y_0) \to (B, b_0)$ be a continuous map, where $Y$ is path-connected and locally path-connected. Then $f$ can be lifted to a continuous map $\tilde{f}: (Y, y_0) \to (E, e_0)$ if and only if:
> $$f_*(\pi_1(Y, y_0)) \subset p_*(\pi_1(E, e_0))$$

> [!theorem] Theorem 79.3 (Conjugacy of Covering Spaces)
> Let $p_1: E_1 \to B$ and $p_2: E_2 \to B$ be path-connected covering spaces of a path-connected, locally path-connected space $B$. These covering spaces are equivalent if and only if the subgroups $H_1 = (p_1)_*(\pi_1(E_1, e_1))$ and $H_2 = (p_2)_*(\pi_1(E_2, e_2))$ are conjugate subgroups within $\pi_1(B, b_0)$.

> [!theorem] Theorem 81.2 (The Group of Covering Transformations)
> Let $p: E \to B$ be the universal covering space of $B$, where $B$ is path-connected and locally path-connected. Then the group of covering transformations $\mathcal{C}(E, p, B)$ is perfectly isomorphic to the fundamental group $\pi_1(B, b_0)$.

> [!theorem] Theorem 81.3 (Quotients by Discontinuous Actions)
> Let a group $G$ act properly discontinuously on a path-connected, locally path-connected space $X$. Let $X/G$ be the orbit space, and let $p: X \to X/G$ be the quotient map. Then $p$ is a regular covering map, and $G$ is its group of covering transformations. If $X$ is simply connected, then $\pi_1(X/G) \cong G$.

> [!theorem] Theorem 82.1 (Existence of the Universal Covering Space)
> A space $B$ has a universal covering space if and only if $B$ is path-connected, locally path-connected, and semilocally simply connected.

> [!theorem] Theorem 82.2 (The Classification Theorem for Covering Spaces)
> Let $B$ be path-connected, locally path-connected, and semilocally simply connected. Then there is a bijective correspondence between the equivalence classes of path-connected covering spaces of $B$ and the conjugacy classes of subgroups of $\pi_1(B, b_0)$.
