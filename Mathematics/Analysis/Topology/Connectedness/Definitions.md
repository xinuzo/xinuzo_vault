

> [!tip] 23.1 Separation
> Let $X$ be a topological space. A separation of $X$ is a pair $U, V$ of disjoint nonempty open subsets of $X$ whose union is $X$.

> [!tip] 23.2 Connected Space
> A topological space $X$ is said to be connected if there does not exist a separation of $X$.

> [!tip] 24.1 Linear Continuum
> A simply ordered set $L$ having more than one element is called a linear continuum if:
> 1. $L$ has the least upper bound property.
> 2. If $x < y$, there exists $z$ such that $x < z < y$.

> [!tip] 24.2 Path
> Given points $x$ and $y$ of the space $X$, a path in $X$ from $x$ to $y$ is a continuous map $f: [a, b] \to X$ of some closed interval in the real line into $X$, such that $f(a) = x$ and $f(b) = y$.

> [!tip] 24.3 Path-Connected Space
> A space $X$ is said to be path-connected if every pair of points of $X$ can be joined by a path in $X$.

> [!tip] 25.1 Connected Component
> Given $X$, define an equivalence relation by setting $x \sim y$ if there is a connected subspace of $X$ containing both $x$ and $y$. The equivalence classes are called the connected components (or simply components) of $X$.

> [!tip] 25.2 Path Component
> Define an equivalence relation on $X$ by setting $x \sim y$ if there is a path in $X$ from $x$ to $y$. The equivalence classes are called the path components of $X$.

> [!tip] 25.3 Locally Connected
> A space $X$ is said to be locally connected at $x$ if for every neighborhood $U$ of $x$, there is a connected neighborhood $V$ of $x$ contained in $U$. If $X$ is locally connected at each of its points, it is said simply to be locally connected.

> [!tip] 25.4 Locally Path-Connected
> A space $X$ is said to be locally path-connected at $x$ if for every neighborhood $U$ of $x$, there is a path-connected neighborhood $V$ of $x$ contained in $U$. If it is locally path-connected at each of its points, then it is said to be locally path-connected.

> [!tip] 26.1 Open Cover
> A collection $\mathcal{A}$ of subsets of a space $X$ is said to cover $X$, or to be a covering of $X$, if the union of the elements of $\mathcal{A}$ is equal to $X$. It is called an open covering of $X$ if its elements are open subsets of $X$.

> [!tip] 26.2 Compact Space
> A space $X$ is said to be compact if every open covering $\mathcal{A}$ of $X$ contains a finite subcollection that also covers $X$.

> [!tip] 26.3 Finite Intersection Property
> A collection $\mathcal{C}$ of subsets of $X$ is said to have the finite intersection property if for every finite subcollection $\{C_1, \dots, C_n\}$ of $\mathcal{C}$, the intersection $C_1 \cap \dots \cap C_n$ is nonempty.

> [!tip] 28.1 Limit Point Compactness
> A space $X$ is said to be limit point compact if every infinite subset of $X$ has a limit point.

> [!tip] 28.2 Sequentially Compact
> A space $X$ is said to be sequentially compact if every sequence of points of $X$ has a convergent subsequence.

> [!tip] 29.1 Locally Compact
> A space $X$ is said to be locally compact at $x$ if there is some neighborhood $U$ of $x$ such that $\overline{U}$ is compact. If $X$ is locally compact at each of its points, $X$ is said simply to be locally compact.
