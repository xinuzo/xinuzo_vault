

> [!tip] 38.1 Compactification
> A compactification of a space $X$ is a compact Hausdorff space $Y$ containing $X$ as a subspace such that the closure of $X$ in $Y$ is all of $Y$ (i.e., $X$ is dense in $Y$). Two compactifications $Y_1$ and $Y_2$ of $X$ are said to be equivalent if there is a homeomorphism $h: Y_1 \to Y_2$ such that $h(x) = x$ for every $x \in X$.

> [!tip] 38.2 The Stone-Čech Compactification
> For a completely regular space $X$, the Stone-Čech compactification of $X$, denoted $\beta(X)$, is a specific compactification constructed by embedding $X$ into a product space $[0, 1]^J$, where $J$ is the index set of all continuous functions from $X$ into $[0, 1]$. It is characterized by its universal extension property.

> [!tip] 38.3 Evaluation Map
> Given a class of continuous functions $\{f_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in J}$ where $f_\alpha : X \to X_\alpha$, the evaluation map $e: X \to \prod_{\alpha \in J} X_\alpha$ is defined by the equation $e(x) = (f_\alpha(x))_{\alpha \in J}$.
