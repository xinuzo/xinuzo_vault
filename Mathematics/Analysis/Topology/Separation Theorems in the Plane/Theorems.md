# Theorems.md

> [!theorem] Lemma 61.1
> Let $A$ be an arc in the sphere $S^2$. Then $S^2 - A$ is connected. (An arc does not separate the sphere).

> [!theorem] Theorem 61.1 (The Jordan Separation Theorem)
> Let $C$ be a simple closed curve in $S^2$. Then the complement $S^2 - C$ has exactly two connected components, and $C$ is the topological boundary of each component.

> [!theorem] Theorem 62.1 (Invariance of Domain)
> Let $U$ be an open subset of $\mathbb{R}^2$, and let $f: U \to \mathbb{R}^2$ be a continuous, injective map. Then the image $f(U)$ is an open subset of $\mathbb{R}^2$, and $f$ is a homeomorphism from $U$ onto $f(U)$.

> [!theorem] Corollary 62.2
> Let $U$ and $V$ be open subsets of $\mathbb{R}^2$. If $f: U \to V$ is a continuous, bijective map, then $f$ is a homeomorphism.

> [!theorem] Theorem 63.4 (The Jordan Curve Theorem)
> Let $C$ be a simple closed curve in $\mathbb{R}^2$. Then $\mathbb{R}^2 - C$ has exactly two connected components, one of which is bounded and the other unbounded. Furthermore, $C$ is the topological boundary of each component.

> [!theorem] Theorem 64.1
> A graph can be imbedded in the plane $\mathbb{R}^2$ if and only if it can be imbedded in the sphere $S^2$.

> [!theorem] Theorem 64.2
> The complete graph on five vertices, $K_5$, cannot be imbedded in the plane.

> [!theorem] Theorem 64.3
> The utilities graph (the complete bipartite graph $K_{3,3}$) cannot be imbedded in the plane.
