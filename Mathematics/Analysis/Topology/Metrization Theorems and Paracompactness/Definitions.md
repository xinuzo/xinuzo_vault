

> [!tip] 39.1 Locally Finite Collection
> Let $X$ be a topological space. A collection $\mathcal{A}$ of subsets of $X$ is said to be locally finite in $X$ if every point $x$ of $X$ has a neighborhood that intersects only finitely many elements of $\mathcal{A}$.

> [!tip] 39.2 Countably Locally Finite ($\sigma$-locally finite)
> A collection $\mathcal{B}$ of subsets of $X$ is said to be countably locally finite (or $\sigma$-locally finite) if it can be represented as the countable union of collections $\mathcal{B}_n$, each of which is locally finite.

> [!tip] 41.1 Refinement
> Let $\mathcal{A}$ be a collection of subsets of the space $X$. A collection $\mathcal{B}$ of subsets of $X$ is said to be a refinement of $\mathcal{A}$ (or is said to refine $\mathcal{A}$) if for each element $B \in \mathcal{B}$, there is an element $A \in \mathcal{A}$ such that $B \subset A$. If the elements of $\mathcal{B}$ are open sets, we call it an open refinement; if they are closed, a closed refinement.

> [!tip] 41.2 Paracompact Space
> A space $X$ is said to be paracompact if every open covering $\mathcal{A}$ of $X$ has a locally finite open refinement $\mathcal{B}$ that covers $X$. 

> [!tip] 42.1 Locally Metrizable
> A space $X$ is locally metrizable if every point $x \in X$ has a neighborhood $U$ that is metrizable in the subspace topology.
