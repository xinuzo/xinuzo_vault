
> [!theorem] Theorem 68.1 (Universal Mapping Property of Free Products)
> Let $G_1$ and $G_2$ be groups, and let $i_1: G_1 \to G_1 * G_2$ and $i_2: G_2 \to G_1 * G_2$ be the standard inclusions. Given any group $H$ and any homomorphisms $h_1: G_1 \to H$ and $h_2: G_2 \to H$, there exists a unique homomorphism $h: G_1 * G_2 \to H$ such that $h \circ i_1 = h_1$ and $h \circ i_2 = h_2$.

> [!theorem] Theorem 70.1 (The Seifert-van Kampen Theorem - Classic Version)
> Let $X = U \cup V$, where $U$ and $V$ are open sets in $X$. Assume $U, V$, and the intersection $U \cap V$ are all path-connected. Let $x_0 \in U \cap V$ be the base point. Then the homomorphisms induced by inclusion, $i_*: \pi_1(U, x_0) \to \pi_1(X, x_0)$ and $j_*: \pi_1(V, x_0) \to \pi_1(X, x_0)$, induce a surjective homomorphism:
> $$\Phi: \pi_1(U, x_0) * \pi_1(V, x_0) \to \pi_1(X, x_0)$$
> Furthermore, the kernel of $\Phi$ is the smallest normal subgroup generated by all elements of the form $i_{UV*}(\omega) (j_{UV*}(\omega))^{-1}$, where $\omega \in \pi_1(U \cap V, x_0)$ and $i_{UV}, j_{UV}$ are the inclusions of $U \cap V$ into $U$ and $V$. 
> *(In group presentation terms: $\pi_1(X)$ is the amalgamated free product of $\pi_1(U)$ and $\pi_1(V)$ over $\pi_1(U \cap V)$).*

> [!theorem] Theorem 70.2 (Seifert-van Kampen - Simply Connected Intersection)
> Let $X = U \cup V$, where $U$ and $V$ are open in $X$. Assume $U, V,$ and $U \cap V$ are path-connected. If $U \cap V$ is simply connected ($\pi_1(U \cap V)$ is trivial), then:
> $$\pi_1(X) \cong \pi_1(U) * \pi_1(V)$$

> [!theorem] Theorem 71.1 (Fundamental Group of a Wedge of Circles)
> Let $X$ be the wedge of $n$ circles (the figure-eight space if $n=2$). Then the fundamental group of $X$ is the free group on exactly $n$ generators.

> [!theorem] Theorem 72.1 (Attaching a 2-Cell)
> Let $X$ be a path-connected space, and let $Y$ be obtained by attaching a 2-dimensional cell (a solid disk $B^2$) to $X$ via a continuous map $f: S^1 \to X$ along its boundary. Then the fundamental group $\pi_1(Y)$ is isomorphic to the quotient of $\pi_1(X)$ by the smallest normal subgroup containing the loop class $[f]$. *(Geometrically: attaching a disk "kills" the loop forming its boundary).*

> [!theorem] Theorem 73.1 (Fundamental Group of the Torus)
> The fundamental group of the torus $T = S^1 \times S^1$ is isomorphic to the free abelian group on two generators, $\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}$. It has the presentation $\langle \alpha, \beta \mid \alpha \beta \alpha^{-1} \beta^{-1} \rangle$.

> [!theorem] Theorem 73.2 (Fundamental Group of the Projective Plane)
> The fundamental group of the real projective plane $\mathbb{R}P^2$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_2$ (the cyclic group of order 2). It has the presentation $\langle \alpha \mid \alpha^2 \rangle$.
