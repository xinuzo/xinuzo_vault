# Theorems.md

> [!theorem] Theorem 43.1
> Every compact metric space is complete.

> [!theorem] Theorem 43.7 (Completion of a Metric Space)
> Let $(X, d)$ be a metric space. There exists a complete metric space $(Y, D)$ and an isometric embedding $h: X \to Y$ such that $h(X)$ is dense in $Y$. The space $Y$ is uniquely determined up to isometry.

> [!theorem] Theorem 45.1
> A metric space $(X, d)$ is compact if and only if it is complete and totally bounded.

> [!theorem] Theorem 46.1 (Uniform Limit Theorem)
> Let $f_n: X \to Y$ be a sequence of continuous functions from the topological space $X$ to the metric space $Y$. If the sequence $(f_n)$ converges uniformly to $f$, then $f$ is continuous.

> [!theorem] Theorem 46.7
> Let $X$ be a topological space and $Y$ a metric space. On the space $C(X,Y)$, the topology of compact convergence is finer than the topology of pointwise convergence, and the uniform topology is finer than the topology of compact convergence.

> [!theorem] Theorem 46.8
> Let $X$ be a topological space and $Y$ a metric space. If $X$ is compact, then the topology of compact convergence on $C(X,Y)$ is identical to the uniform topology.

> [!theorem] Theorem 47.1
> Let $X$ be a space and $Y$ a metric space. On the set $C(X,Y)$, the compact-open topology and the topology of compact convergence coincide.

> [!theorem] Theorem 48.2 (Baire Category Theorem)
> If $X$ is a compact Hausdorff space or a complete metric space, then $X$ is a Baire space.

> [!theorem] Theorem 48.4
> Every $G_\delta$ set in a Baire space is itself a Baire space in the subspace topology.
