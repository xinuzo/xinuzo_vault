> [!theorem] Theorem 30.1
> A subspace of a first-countable space is first-countable, and a countable product of first-countable spaces is first-countable. A subspace of a second-countable space is second-countable, and a countable product of second-countable spaces is second-countable.

> [!theorem] Theorem 30.3
> Suppose that $X$ has a countable basis (is second-countable). Then:
> 1. Every open covering of $X$ contains a countable subcollection covering $X$ (implies Lindelöf).
> 2. There exists a countable dense subset of $X$ (implies separable).

> [!theorem] Lemma 31.1 (Equivalence for Regularity)
> Let $X$ be a topological space; let one-point sets be closed in $X$. Then $X$ is regular if and only if given a point $x$ of $X$ and a neighborhood $U$ of $x$, there is a neighborhood $V$ of $x$ such that $\overline{V} \subset U$.

> [!theorem] Lemma 31.2 (Equivalence for Normality)
> Let $X$ be a topological space; let one-point sets be closed in $X$. Then $X$ is normal if and only if given a closed set $A$ and an open set $U$ containing $A$, there is an open set $V$ containing $A$ such that $\overline{V} \subset U$.

> [!theorem] Theorem 31.4
> A subspace of a regular space is regular. A product of regular spaces is regular.

> [!theorem] Theorem 32.1
> Every regular space with a countable basis is normal.

> [!theorem] Theorem 32.2
> Every metrizable space is normal.

> [!theorem] Theorem 32.3
> Every compact Hausdorff space is normal.

> [!theorem] Theorem 33.1 (The Urysohn Lemma)
> Let $X$ be a normal space; let $A$ and $B$ be disjoint closed subsets of $X$. Let $[a, b]$ be a closed interval in the real line. Then there exists a continuous map $f: X \to [a, b]$ such that $f(x) = a$ for every $x \in A$, and $f(x) = b$ for every $x \in B$.

> [!theorem] Theorem 33.2
> Every completely regular space is regular. Every normal space is completely regular.

> [!theorem] Theorem 34.1 (The Urysohn Metrization Theorem)
> Every regular space $X$ with a countable basis is metrizable.

> [!theorem] Theorem 35.1 (The Tietze Extension Theorem)
> Let $X$ be a normal space; let $A$ be a closed subspace of $X$.
> 1. Any continuous map of $A$ into the closed interval $[a, b]$ of $\mathbb{R}$ may be extended to a continuous map of all of $X$ into $[a, b]$.
> 2. Any continuous map of $A$ into $\mathbb{R}$ may be extended to a continuous map of all of $X$ into $\mathbb{R}$.
