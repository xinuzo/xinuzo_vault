>[!tips] 9.1 Geodesic
>A curve $\gamma$ on a surface is a geodesic if its geodesic curvature $\kappa_g$ is zero everywhere.
>Intuitively, the acceleration vector $\ddot{\gamma}$ is always perpendicular to the surface (parallel to the normal $\mathbf{N}$).
>Geodesics are the "straight lines" of the surface.

>[!tips] 9.2 Geodesic Equations
>A system of second-order differential equations satisfied by the coordinates $(u(t), v(t))$ of a geodesic.
>$$\ddot{u} + \Gamma_{11}^1 \dot{u}^2 + 2\Gamma_{12}^1 \dot{u}\dot{v} + \Gamma_{22}^1 \dot{v}^2 = 0$$
>$$\ddot{v} + \Gamma_{11}^2 \dot{u}^2 + 2\Gamma_{12}^2 \dot{u}\dot{v} + \Gamma_{22}^2 \dot{v}^2 = 0$$
>where $\Gamma_{ij}^k$ are the Christoffel symbols (depending only on $E, F, G$).
