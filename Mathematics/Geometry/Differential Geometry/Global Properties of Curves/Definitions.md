>[!tips] 3.1 Simple Closed Curve
>A curve $\gamma: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}^2$ is a simple closed curve with period $T$ if:
>1. $\gamma$ is $T$-periodic ($\gamma(t+T) = \gamma(t)$).
>2. [cite_start]$\gamma(t_1) \neq \gamma(t_2)$ for any $t_1, t_2 \in [0, T)$ with $t_1 \neq t_2$ (no self-intersections). 

>[!tips] 3.1 Interior
>The bounded region enclosed by a simple closed curve $\gamma$ is called its interior, denoted $int(\gamma)$. [cite_start]The unbounded region is the exterior. 

>[!tips] 3.1 Orientation
>[cite_start]A simple closed curve is positively oriented if the interior is on the left as one moves along the curve (counter-clockwise). 

>[!tips] 3.3 Convex Curve
>[cite_start]A simple closed curve $\gamma$ is convex if its interior $int(\gamma)$ is a convex set (the straight line segment joining any two points in the interior is contained entirely within the interior). 

>[!tips] 3.3 Vertex
>[cite_start]A vertex of a plane curve is a point where its signed curvature $\kappa_s$ has a stationary point, i.e., where $\frac{d\kappa_s}{dt} = 0$.
