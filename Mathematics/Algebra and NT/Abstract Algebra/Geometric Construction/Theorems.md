>[!tips] Theorem 52.1 Constructibility Criterion
>A real number $r$ is constructible iff there is a finite sequence of subfields of $\mathbb{R}$, $\mathbb{Q} = F_0 \subset F_1 \subset \dots \subset F_k$, such that $r \in F_k$ and each $F_i = F_{i-1}(\sqrt{a_{i-1}})$ for some positive $a_{i-1} \in F_{i-1}$.

>[!tips] Theorem 53.1 Non-Constructibility
>If a cubic polynomial with rational coefficients has no rational root, then it has no constructible root.

>[!tips] 53 Impossible Constructions
>1. **Doubling the Cube**: Impossible because $\sqrt[3]{2}$ is not constructible ($[\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2}):\mathbb{Q}] = 3$, not a power of 2).
>2. **Trisecting the Angle**: Impossible for general angles (e.g., $60^\circ$) because it requires constructing a root of a cubic polynomial irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$.
>3. **Squaring the Circle**: Impossible because $\pi$ is transcendental (Lindemann, 1882), hence not algebraic, and thus not constructible.
