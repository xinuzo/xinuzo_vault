
## 📅 Dates: The Special Readings (日付 - Hizuke)

The first ten days of the month and a few others have special readings that must be memorized.

- **1日 (tsuitachi)** - 1st
    
- **2日 (futsuka)** - 2nd
    
- **3日 (mikka)** - 3rd
    
- **4日 (yokka)** - 4th
    
- **5日 (itsuka)** - 5th
    
- **6日 (muika)** - 6th
    
- **7日 (nanoka)** - 7th
    
- **8日 (yōka)** - 8th
    
- **9日 (kokonoka)** - 9th
    
- **10日 (tōka)** - 10th
    

> [!TIP] Other Special Days
> 
> - **14日 (jūyokka)** - 14th
>     
> - **20日 (hatsuka)** - 20th
>     
> - 24日 (nijūyokka) - 24th
>     
>     For all other days, use the standard number + 日 (nichi). Example: 11日 (jūichi-nichi).
>

## 👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 Family & Relatives (家族 - Kazoku)

The word you use for a family member changes depending on whether you are talking about **your own family** to an outsider (humble) or **someone else's family** (respectful).

|English|My Family (Humble)|Someone Else's (Respectful)|
|---|---|---|
|Mother|**母 (haha)**|**お母さん (okāsan)**|
|Father|**父 (chichi)**|**お父さん (otōsan)**|
|Older Sister|**姉 (ane)**|**お姉さん (onēsan)**|
|Older Brother|**兄 (ani)**|**お兄さん (oniisan)**|
|Younger Sister|**妹 (imōto)**|**妹さん (imōto-san)**|
|Younger Brother|**弟 (otōto)**|**弟さん (otōto-san)**|
|Grandmother|**祖母 (sobo)**|**おばあさん (obāsan)**|
|Grandfather|**祖父 (sofu)**|**おじいさん (ojiisan)**|
|Wife|**妻 (tsuma)** / **家内 (kanai)**|**奥さん (okusan)**|
|Husband|**夫 (otto)** / **主人 (shujin)**|**ご主人 (go-shujin)**|

> [!NOTE] Uchi-Soto Concept
> 
> This is based on the cultural concept of 内 (uchi - inside) and 外 (soto - outside). When speaking to outsiders, you humbly refer to your "inside" group (your family).

## 🔢 Counters (助数詞 - Josūshi)

Japanese uses specific counter words when counting objects, people, or animals.

|Counter|What it Counts|Examples|
|---|---|---|
|**〜つ (tsu)**|General items (when you don't know the specific counter). Only goes up to 10.|`一つ (hitotsu)`, `二つ (futatsu)`|
|**〜人 (nin/ri)**|People|`一人 (hitori)`, `二人 (futari)`, `三人 (sannin)`|
|**〜匹 (hiki)**|Small animals (cats, dogs, fish)|`一匹 (ippiki)`, `二匹 (nihiki)`, `三匹 (sanbiki)`|
|**〜本 (hon)**|Long, cylindrical objects (pens, bottles, trees)|`一本 (ippon)`, `二本 (nihon)`, `三本 (sanbon)`|
|**〜枚 (mai)**|Thin, flat objects (paper, plates, shirts)|`一枚 (ichimai)`, `二枚 (nimai)`|
|**〜冊 (satsu)**|Bound objects (books, magazines, notebooks)|`一冊 (issatsu)`, `二冊 (nisatsu)`|
|**〜台 (dai)**|Machines and vehicles (cars, computers, TVs)|`一台 (ichidai)`, `二台 (nidai)`|
|**〜個 (ko)**|Small, roundish objects (apples, eggs)|`一個 (ikko)`, `二個 (niko)`|

> [!WARNING] Sound Changes
> 
> Be careful of sound changes! For example, hon can become pon or bon depending on the number before it.

## 🙇‍♂️ Greetings & Keigo (挨拶と敬語 - Aisatsu to Keigo)

### Common Greetings (Aisatsu)

- **お疲れ様です (Otsukaresama desu):** A universal greeting in the workplace. Means everything from "Hello" to "Good work" to "Goodbye." Used to acknowledge someone's hard work.
    
- **よろしくお願いします (Yoroshiku onegaishimasu):** "Nice to meet you," "I look forward to working with you," "Thank you in advance." Used at the beginning of a new relationship or task.
    
- **お世話になっております (Osewa ni natte orimasu):** "Thank you for your continued support." A standard greeting for clients or people you have an ongoing relationship with.
    

### Keigo Basics (敬語)

Keigo (honorific language) has three main categories:

1. **尊敬語 (Sonkeigo):** Respectful Language. Used when speaking _about_ a superior's actions. It elevates the other person.
    
2. **謙譲語 (Kenjōgo):** Humble Language. Used when speaking _about your own_ actions to a superior. It lowers yourself.
    
3. **丁寧語 (Teineigo):** Polite Language. The standard polite form using `〜です` and `〜ます`.
    

|Plain Form|Polite Form (Teineigo)|Respectful (Sonkeigo)|Humble (Kenjōgo)|
|---|---|---|---|
|**行く (iku)** - to go|**行きます (ikimasu)**|**いらっしゃる (irassharu)**|**参る (mairu)** / **伺う (ukagau)**|
|**食べる (taberu)** - to eat|**食べます (tabemasu)**|**召し上がる (meshiagaru)**|**いただく (itadaku)**|
|**する (suru)** - to do|**します (shimasu)**|**なさる (nasaru)**|**いたす (itasu)**|

## ↔️ Transitive & Intransitive Verbs (他動詞と自動詞)

Many verbs come in pairs. One describes an action done by someone (transitive), and the other describes something happening on its own (intransitive).

|Transitive (Action by someone - uses `を`)|Intransitive (Event - uses `が`)|Meaning|
|---|---|---|
|**開ける (akeru)** - to open (something)|**開く (aku)** - (something) opens|To open|
|**閉める (shimeru)** - to close (something)|**閉まる (shimaru)** - (something) closes|To close|
|**入れる (ireru)** - to put (something) in|**入る (hairu)** - (something) enters|To put in/enter|
|**出す (dasu)** - to take (something) out|**出る (deru)** - (something) comes out|To take out/exit|
|**つける (tsukeru)** - to turn (something) on|**つく (tsuku)** - (something) turns on|To turn on|
|**消す (kesu)** - to turn (something) off|**消える (kieru)** - (something) turns off|To turn off|

- **Example (Transitive):** 私がドア**を**開けます。 (Watashi ga doa **o** akemasu.) - **I** open the door.
    
- **Example (Intransitive):** ドア**が**開きます。 (Doa **ga** akimasu.) - The door opens.
