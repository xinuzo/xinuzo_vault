
## Ⅰ. Core Grammar Theory (基本文法)

### Verbs (動詞 - dōshi)

Japanese verbs are divided into three groups, which determine how they conjugate.

#### **Group 1: Godan Verbs (五段動詞)**

Also known as "u-verbs." This is the largest group. They typically end with a `~u` sound syllable that is NOT `-iru` or `-eru`.

- **How to Spot:** Any verb ending in `~う, ~く, ~ぐ, ~す, ~つ, ~ぬ, ~ぶ, ~む, ~る` where the vowel before the `る` is NOT `i` or `e`.
    
- **Examples:** `会う (au)`, `書く (kaku)`, `話す (hanasu)`, `待つ (matsu)`, `飲む (nomu)`
    
- **Important Exceptions:** Some verbs look like Group 2 but are actually Group 1.
    
    - `帰る (kaeru)` - to return
        
    - `入る (hairu)` - to enter
        
    - `走る (hashiru)` - to run
        
    - `知る (shiru)` - to know
        

#### **Group 2: Ichidan Verbs (一段動詞)**

Also known as "ru-verbs." This is the simplest group to conjugate.

- **How to Spot:** Verbs that end in `-iru` or `-eru`.
    
- **How to Conjugate:** Simply drop the final `~る (ru)` and add the ending.
    
- **Examples:**
    
    - `食べる (taberu)` → `食べます (tabemasu)`, `食べて (tabete)`
        
    - `見る (miru)` → `見ます (mimasu)`, `見て (mite)`
        

#### **Group 3: Irregular Verbs (不規則動詞)**

There are only two main irregular verbs that must be memorized.

- **`する (suru)`** - to do
    
- **`来る (kuru)`** - to come

### Adjectives (形容詞 - keiyōshi)

Japanese has two types of adjectives with different conjugation rules.

#### **I-Adjectives (い形容詞)**

These words end in `い (i)`.

- **Examples:** `大きい (ōkii)`, `寒い (samui)`, `楽しい (tanoshii)`
    
- **Conjugation:**
    
    - **Present Negative:** `寒くない (samukunai)` - (drop `い`, add `くない`)
        
    - **Past:** `寒かった (samukatta)` - (drop `い`, add `かった`)
        
    - **Past Negative:** `寒くなかった (samukunakatta)`
        

#### **Na-Adjectives (な形容詞)**

These words act like nouns and do not end in `い` (though some exceptions exist, like `きれい`).

- **Examples:** `元気 (genki)`, `静か (shizuka)`, `便利 (benri)`
    
- **Conjugation:**
    
    - **To describe a noun:** Add `な (na)`. → `元気**な**人 (genki **na** hito)` - an energetic person.
        
    - **To end a sentence (polite):** Add `です/でした`. → `元気です (genki desu)`, `元気でした (genki deshita)`
        
    - **Negative:** `元気じゃない (genki ja nai)`, `元気ではありません (genki dewa arimasen)`

## Ⅱ. Situational Phrase Toolkit (場面別フレーズ)

### Expressing Gratitude (感謝 - kansha)

|Phrase|Nuance / When to Use|
|---|---|
|**ありがとう (arigatō)**|Casual "Thanks." For friends and family.|
|**ありがとうございます (arigatō gozaimasu)**|Standard polite "Thank you." For strangers, shop staff, colleagues.|
|**どうもありがとうございます (dōmo arigatō gozaimasu)**|"Thank you very much." A more polite and heartfelt version.|
|**感謝します (kansha shimasu)**|"I am grateful." More formal and expresses a deeper feeling of gratitude.|
|**恐れ入ります (osore irimasu)**|Extremely polite business phrase. "I'm indebted to you" or "Thank you for your trouble."|

### Expressing Tiredness (疲れ - tsukare)

|Phrase|Nuance / When to Use|
|---|---|
|**疲れました (tsukaremashita)**|"I'm tired." The standard phrase for physical or mental exhaustion after an activity.|
|**眠いです (nemui desu)**|"I'm sleepy." Specifically for the feeling of wanting to sleep.|
|**だるいです (darui desu)**|"I feel sluggish/lethargic." A feeling of heavy-bodied weariness, often when you are sick.|
|**しんどいです (shindoi desu)**|"(This is) exhausting/tough." A stronger, more emotionally draining kind of tiredness. Common in Kansai dialect but used everywhere.|

### Telling a Story (物語 - monogatari)

> [!TIP] Storytelling Structure
> 
> Use these templates to structure a personal story.

- **Opening:**
    
    - `この間、(kono aida,)` - "The other day,"
        
    - `昔、(mukashi,)` - "A while ago,"
        
    - `〜の時、(〜no toki,)` - "When I was...," (e.g., `学生の時` - when I was a student)
        
- **Connecting Phrases:**
    
    - `それで、(sorede,)` - "And so," / "Then,"
        
    - `すると、(suruto,)` - "And then, suddenly," (implies a surprising next event)
        
    - `実は、(jitsu wa,)` - "Actually," / "To tell you the truth,"
        
- **Closing:**
    
    - `という経験がありました。(to iu keiken ga arimashita.)` - "I had an experience like that."
        
    - `本当にびっくりしました。(hontō ni bikkuri shimashita.)` - "I was really surprised."
        
    - `とても勉強になりました。(totemo benkyō ni narimashita.)` - "It was a great learning experience."
        

### Making a Request (依頼 - irai)

The nuance changes drastically with the politeness level.

|Phrase (using `手伝う` - tetsudau, to help)|Nuance / Politeness Level|
|---|---|
|**手伝って (tetsudatte)**|**Command.** Very casual, can be rude. For close friends/family only.|
|**手伝ってください (tetsudatte kudasai)**|**Standard Polite Request.** The first one you should learn. Safe for most situations.|
|**手伝ってくれますか (tetsudatte kuremasu ka)**|**"Will you help me?"** A slightly softer, more personal request.|
|**手伝ってもらえますか (tetsudatte moraemasu ka)**|**"Could I receive the favor of you helping?"** Very polite. Focus is on you receiving the action.|
|**手伝っていただけませんか (tetsudatte itadakemasen ka)**|**"Would it be possible for me to humbly receive your help?"** Extremely polite and humble. Use with superiors, clients, or when asking a big favor.|

### Interview Phrases (面接 - mensetsu)

- **Self-Introduction:**
    
    - `〜と申します。(〜to mōshimasu.)` - "My name is..." (Humble form of `言います`)
        
    - `本日はよろしくお願いいたします。(honjitsu wa yoroshiku onegai itashimasu.)` - "Thank you for your time today." (Very formal)
        
- **Strengths & Weaknesses:**
    
    - **Strength:** `私の強みは〜です。(watashi no tsuyomi wa 〜 desu.)` - "My strength is..."
        
    - **Weakness:** `私の課題は〜です。(watashi no kadai wa 〜 desu.)` - "My challenge/area for improvement is..."
        
        > [!NOTE] It is common to frame weaknesses as "challenges" (`課題`) rather than "weak points" (`弱み` - yowami) to sound more positive and growth-oriented.
        
- **Expressing Aspiration:**
    
    - `貴社で私の〜というスキルを活かしたいです。(kisha de watashi no 〜 to iu sukiru o ikashitai desu.)` - "I want to utilize my skill of... at your company."
        
        > [!NOTE] `貴社` (kisha) is the respectful word for "your company" in writing and formal speech.
