
## Ⅰ. Particle Mistakes (助詞の間違い)

Particles are the glue of Japanese sentences, and they are the most common source of errors.

### 1. Confusing は (wa) and が (ga)

This is the most classic mistake. They do not both simply mean "is" or "am."

> [!DANGER] AVOID (Unnatural)
> 
> 私が学生です。 (Watashi ga gakusei desu.)

> [!SUCCESS] OBEY (Natural)
> 
> 私は学生です。 (Watashi wa gakusei desu.)

**The Rule (ルール):**

- **は (wa)** is the **Topic Marker**. It introduces the main theme of the conversation. "As for me..." or "Speaking of me..." It's for information the listener already knows about or is being introduced as the main subject.
    
- **が (ga)** is the **Subject Identifier**. It identifies new or specific information. It answers the question "who?" or "what?".
    

**Simple Tip (ヒント):**

- Use **は (wa)** for general statements: `猫**は**かわいいです。` (Neko **wa** kawaii desu.) - "Cats are cute."
    
- Use **が (ga)** for specific observations or preferences: `猫**が**好きです。` (Neko **ga** suki desu.) - "**It is cats** that I like."
    

### 2. Confusing に (ni) and で (de) for Location

Both can mean "in," "at," or "on," but they are not interchangeable.

> [!DANGER] AVOID
> 
> 図書館**に**勉強します。 (Toshokan ni benkyō shimasu.)

> [!SUCCESS] OBEY
> 
> 図書館**で**勉強します。 (Toshokan de benkyō shimasu.)

**The Rule (ルール):**

- **に (ni)** marks a point of **existence or destination**. Use it with verbs like `います` (imasu - to exist), `あります` (arimasu - to exist), `住んでいます` (sundeimasu - to live), and `行きます` (ikimasu - to go). It's about a static state.
    
- **で (de)** marks the **location of an action**. If a dynamic verb (doing something) is happening in a place, you use `で`.
    

**Simple Tip (ヒント):** Ask yourself: "Is something _happening_ at the location?"

- If yes → **で (de)**. (e.g., `レストラン**で**食べます。` - I eat **at** the restaurant.)
    
- If no (it's just there) → **に (ni)**. (e.g., `レストラン**に**います。` - I am **at** the restaurant.)
    

### 3. Using を (o) instead of が (ga) with Potential Verbs

When you say you "can do" something, the particle often changes.

> [!DANGER] AVOID (Grammatically OK, but less natural)
> 
> 日本語**を**話せます。 (Nihongo o hanasemasu.)

> [!SUCCESS] OBEY (More common and natural)
> 
> 日本語**が**話せます。 (Nihongo ga hanasemasu.)

The Rule (ルール):

When using the potential form of a verb (〜ことができる or 〜(ら)れる), the object of the ability is often marked with が (ga) instead of を (o). This shifts the focus from the action itself to the ability.

## Ⅱ. Verb Mistakes (動詞の間違い)

### 1. Confusing Transitive and Intransitive Verbs

This is a huge hurdle. Many verbs come in pairs: one for an action you do (transitive), and one for something that happens on its own (intransitive).

> [!DANGER] AVOID
> 
> ドア**を開けた**。 (Doa o aketa.) - This means "I opened the door." It's wrong if you want to say "The door opened."

> [!SUCCESS] OBEY
> 
> ドア**が開いた**。 (Doa ga aita.) - "The door opened."

**The Rule (ルール):**

- **Transitive (他動詞 - tadōshi):** An action done by a "doer." It takes a direct object with **を (o)**. (e.g., `電気**を**つけます` - I turn on the light).
    
- **Intransitive (自動詞 - jidōshi):** An event that happens on its own. The thing it happens to is the subject, marked with **が (ga)**. (e.g., `電気**が**つきます` - The light turns on).
    

**Simple Tip (ヒント):** Always ask: "Is someone _doing_ the action, or is it _happening_?"

### 2. Confusing Giving/Receiving Verbs (くれる, あげる, もらう)

These are based entirely on perspective (who is giving to whom).

> [!DANGER] AVOID
> 
> 友達は私にプレゼントを**あげました**。 (Tomodachi wa watashi ni purezento o agemashita.)

> [!SUCCESS] OBEY
> 
> 友達は私にプレゼントを**くれました**。 (Tomodachi wa watashi ni purezento o kuremashita.)

**The Rule (ルール):**

- **あげる (ageru):** You (or someone in your "in-group") give to an "out-group" person. **(I/We → Them)**
    
- **くれる (kureru):** An "out-group" person gives to you (or your "in-group"). **(They → Me/Us)**
    
- **もらう (morau):** You (or your "in-group") receive something from someone. **(I/We ← Them)** The focus is on the receiver.
    

**Simple Tip (ヒント):** If the action is coming _towards you_ (or your family), use **くれる (kureru)**.

## Ⅲ. Politeness & Natural Phrasing (丁寧さと自然な表現)

### 1. Overusing あなた (anata)

`あなた` does mean "you," but it can sound very direct, impersonal, or even confrontational. In some cases, it's what a wife calls her husband.

> [!DANGER] AVOID
> 
> **あなた**は何歳ですか。 (Anata wa nansai desu ka.)

> [!SUCCESS] OBEY
> 
> **田中さんは**おいくつですか。 (Tanaka-san wa oikutsu desu ka.) - Use their name.
> 
> 失礼ですが、おいくつですか。 (Shitsurei desu ga, oikutsu desu ka.) - Omit the pronoun.

The Rule (ルール):

In Japanese, avoid using あなた when you know the person's name. It's much more polite to use their Name + さん (san). If you don't know their name, it's better to just omit the pronoun entirely if the context is clear.

### 2. Mixing Polite (`〜ます`) and Casual (`〜だ`) Forms

A sentence should be consistent in its level of politeness.

> [!DANGER] AVOID
> 
> 昨日は楽しかった**だ**けど、疲れ**ました**。 (Kinō wa tanoshikatta da kedo, tsukaremashita.)

> [!SUCCESS] OBEY
> 
> 昨日は楽しかった**です**が、疲れ**ました**。 (Kinō wa tanoshikatta desu ga, tsukaremashita.) - Polite
> 
> 昨日は楽しかった**けど**、疲れ**た**。 (Kinō wa tanoshikatta kedo, tsukareta.) - Casual

The Rule (ルール):

Maintain a consistent politeness level throughout a single thought or sentence. If you start with 〜ます, end with 〜ます. If you start casual, stay casual.
